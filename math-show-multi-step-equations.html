Multi-Step Equations

Some equations need more than one step. The easiest way to approach them is to work through the equation in a predictable order: simplify first, then isolate the variable.

Visual 01 · The Big Picture

One equation can have several layers.

Instead of trying to solve everything at once, peel away one layer at a time.

2x + 3 = 11
1
Remove the +3
Undo addition by subtracting 3 from both sides.
2
Remove the ×2
Undo multiplication by dividing both sides by 2.
3
Check the answer
Make sure your value makes the original equation true.
Visual 02 · Step One

Undo addition or subtraction first.

Look at: 2x + 3 = 11

2x + 3 = 11
↓
2x + 3 − 3 = 11 − 3
↓
2x = 8
The +3 disappeared because we subtracted 3 from both sides.
Visual 03 · Step Two

Now undo multiplication.

We are left with: 2x = 8 The 2 is multiplying x.

2x = 8
↓
2x ÷ 2 = 8 ÷ 2
↓
x = 4
The ×2 disappeared because we divided both sides by 2.
Visual 04 · Why This Order?

Think of the equation like layers.

Imagine that x is inside several mathematical layers. We remove the outer layer first.

First layer

Undo addition or subtraction.

2x + 3 → 2x

Second layer

Undo multiplication or division.

2x → x
Visual 05 · Full Example

Let's watch the whole thing happen.

We will solve: 3x − 5 = 16

1
3x − 5 = 16
Start with the original equation.
2
3x − 5 + 5 = 16 + 5
Add 5 to both sides to undo −5.
3
3x = 21
The constant has been removed.
4
3x ÷ 3 = 21 ÷ 3
Divide both sides by 3.
5
x = 7
The variable is isolated.

What comes first?

The NeuralPath method

When an equation looks complicated, don't solve everything at once.

1. Look for addition or subtraction.
2. Undo it on BOTH sides.
3. Look for multiplication or division.
4. Undo it on BOTH sides.
5. Check your answer.
Example: 3x − 5 = 16 → x = 7
