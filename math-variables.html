Understanding Variables

A variable might look intimidating at first, but the idea behind it is actually pretty simple.

Lesson progress 1 of 5
Step 1 · Learn

What is a variable?

A variable is a symbol that represents a value.

In algebra, variables are usually letters such as x, y, or n.

x + 5 = 12

In this equation, we don't know what x is yet. That's why we use a variable.

Think of a variable like a mystery box.

You know something is inside the box, but you don't know exactly what it is. The letter x gives us a way to talk about that unknown value.

Step 2 · See it

Let's find x.

We have:

x + 5 = 12

We want to get x by itself. Since 5 is being added to x, we can subtract 5 from both sides.

x + 5 − 5 = 12 − 5

The 5s cancel on the left. That leaves:

x = 7
Because we want x by itself.

Addition and subtraction are opposite operations. Since the equation adds 5 to x, subtracting 5 reverses that operation.

Step 3 · Try it

Your turn.

Solve the equation:

x + 6 = 10

What is x?

Need another way?

The same idea can be explained in different ways. Choose one that makes more sense to you.

Keep it simple

A variable is a letter standing in for a number we don't know yet. In x + 6 = 10, x is 4.

Show me visually

Imagine 10 blocks. Six of them are already known. The remaining blocks are the unknown amount. That remaining amount is x. So: 10 − 6 = 4.

Give me an analogy

Imagine you have a bag containing an unknown number of pencils. You add 6 more pencils and now you have 10. The bag originally contained 4. That unknown amount is x.

Step-by-step

1. Start with x + 6 = 10.
2. Subtract 6 from both sides.
3. x + 6 − 6 = 10 − 6.
4. The 6s cancel.
5. x = 4.
