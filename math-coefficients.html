Coefficients & Constants

Variables are only one part of an algebraic expression. Let's figure out what the numbers around them are doing.

Lesson progress 2 of 5
Step 1 · Learn

Meet the coefficient.

A coefficient is a number multiplied by a variable.

3x

In 3x, the 3 is the coefficient and x is the variable.

3 Coefficient
Tells us how many x's
x Variable
Represents a value
× Multiplication
3x means 3 × x
Think of it like groups.

If x represents one group, then 3x means three groups of x. The coefficient tells you how many groups you have.

Step 2 · Learn

Now meet the constant.

A constant is a number that does not have a variable attached to it.

3x + 7

Let's identify every part.

3 Coefficient
Multiplies x
x Variable
Represents a value
7 Constant
Has no variable

So in 3x + 7: 3 is the coefficient, x is the variable, and 7 is the constant.

Step 3 · Try it

Find the coefficient.

Look at the expression:

5x + 12

What is the coefficient?

Now try this one:

8x + 3

What is the constant?

Need another way?

Pick an explanation style that works better for your brain right now.

Keep it simple

The coefficient tells you how many times the variable is being used. In 4x, the coefficient is 4. A constant is simply a number without a variable.

Show me visually

Think of x as one box. 4x would be: [x] [x] [x] [x] Four boxes containing x. The 4 is the coefficient.

Give me an analogy

Imagine ordering 4 pizzas. If one pizza represents x, then 4x means four pizzas. The coefficient tells you how many pizzas you have. A constant is like a separate item on the receipt that isn't multiplied by anything.

Step-by-step

1. Find the variable.
2. Look for the number directly multiplying it.
3. That number is the coefficient.
4. Find any number without a variable.
5. That number is the constant.
